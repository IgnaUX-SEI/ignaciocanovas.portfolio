The Story

Not just research. Product direction.

Most researchers get handed a product and asked to validate it. I got handed a product nobody had mapped, a team without a user flow, and an AI agent producing outputs nobody knew how to assess.

The Reinvention Navigator is Accenture's enterprise change management platform. The Change Impact Agent is a new AI-powered feature being built natively inside it — designed to replace a fragmented six-step manual process that practitioners were running across ChatGPT, Copilot, and Excel, with no traceability and no integration with the platform.

My engagement spans three sequential research phases. Each one expanded the scope of what I owned — from validating a feature list, to defining the end-to-end navigation, to designing the research infrastructure that determines whether an AI agent is ready to be committed to client delivery.

The Problem

A six-step manual process. No platform. No traceability.

Change practitioners were extracting change impacts outside of Reinvention Navigator — using whatever tools were available — then importing results by hand.

01Attend workshops — extract change impact signals from conversations not designed for that purpose
02Take notes or get a Copilot summary — OneNote, manual notes, or Copilot
03Synthesize in ChatGPT — paste notes, prompt for extraction, refine across people, process, technology, and data
04Build an Excel — paste ChatGPT output into a CIA Excel template
05Import to RN — upload manually
06Deliver to client

Steps 3, 4, and 5 replaced by the Change Impact Agent.

Phase 01 · Feature Prioritization

Which of 23 features gets built first?

I designed and facilitated a 1-hour Mural-based prioritization workshop with 6 change practitioners across 4 prototype screens and 23 features using a drag-and-drop priority activity.

01
The feature set is validated by lived experience
Participants described the problems before they saw the features. This was not a speculative build.
02
Mandatory features needed defending, not descoping
Four mandatory features received weak vote signal — not because they lacked value, but because participants didn't connect the label to the solution. They had built workarounds for the exact problems these features solve. The evidence layer revealed a comprehension gap, not a value gap. This finding changed how the product team treated the feature set.
03
Two optional features should be reclassified as near-essential
"Group impacts into themes" received unanimous High votes. "Export to Excel / Edit / Re-Import" received strong High signals — Excel remained the working environment for most practitioners.
04
RN-native context changes the value proposition
Some votes were cast before participants understood the tool was RN-native. Early votes had to be treated as directional, not definitive.
"Practitioners were already getting 3.8 out of 5 on output quality from self-built agents. That number became the benchmark Phase 03 is designed to meet or exceed."

Phase 02 · Usability Testing

Is the prototype ready — and what must be resolved before engineering commits?

Before running a single session I tested the prototype myself. It made no sense. The user flow was missing entirely. I mapped it before the sessions began — giving the India team a navigational foundation they could work from. This wasn't a research deliverable. It was a product decision made under time pressure to prevent a failed study.

4 concept testing sessions · 2 days · 10 practitioners across T&O, Rio Tinto, and Belden.

144 atomic observations → 48 per-screen findings → 12 cross-study themes → 13 strategic recommendations

4 Critical Issues — Fix Before Build
SR-01
Redesign the end-to-end flow
Clicking "Create Impact Using AI" led participants to expect an immediate AI interaction — not a dashboard. Fix: remove Batch Overview as entry point. "Create with AI" goes directly to Batch Configuration. Returning users access batches via "View my batches."
SR-03
Resolve terminology before the product is labelled
"Batch" confused participants in 3 of 4 sessions. Recommended: Session upload or Transcript upload instead of "batch." Configure your session or Set up extraction instead of "Batch Configuration."
SR-04
Reorder Batch Configuration before it is built
Sessions 2 and 3 independently identified the same preferred order. Context must be set before documents are uploaded. Recommended: Batch Information → RN Reference Data → Upload Documents → AI Guidance.
SR-05
Collapse the editing view into the table
Participants avoided navigating away from the table in all 4 sessions. Eight interaction patterns identified — accordion row expansion, hover tooltips, slide-out detail panel, evidence markers, conversational AI for field correction.
4 Things That Are Working — Protect in MVP 1
SR-10
AI Rationale + Evidence
Most consistently valued features across all sessions. Bradley: "The combination of confidence level and evidence is what makes this usable — not just a number."
SR-11
The editing view structure
Strongest screen in the prototype. Step-by-step layout, Previous/Next navigation, and Rationale + Evidence proximity to editable fields all worked.
SR-12
The AI Guidance field
Well understood and immediately valued.
SR-13
The batch-per-workshop model
Directly addressed the documented pain point of waiting for all workshops to finish before extracting.

Phase 03 · AI Output Quality Test · Research in progress — Findings due June 4, 2026

Is the AI-generated extraction accurate enough to commit to a real client project?

The quality test introduces a hallucination detection framework built into the assessment methodology — without asking participants to evaluate for hallucinations directly.

5 Accenture practitioners · 45 min sessions · 1-on-1 · screen share · real transcripts

Assessment scored 1–10 where 1 = low quality / low accuracy and 10 = high quality / high accuracy.

DimensionScaleWhat it captures
Field Quality1–10Title, summary, current state, future state accuracy
Mapped Fields1–10Stakeholders, locations, process areas vs. reference data
Impact Rating1–10People, process, technology risk levels
Evidence Traceable?Yes / NoCan they find the source in the transcript? No = hallucination signal
AI Confidence Agree?Yes / NoDoes the AI score match their judgment?
Editing IntentK / E / DKeep, Edit, or Delete — and why
Output Accuracy1–10Overall synthesis score. Given last, after all other columns.
"The K / E / D column is a behavioral variable, not a self-reported one. It captures what the practitioner actually decides to do with each AI-generated impact — without priming them to look for errors."
Three Failure Modes — Editing Intent as Hallucination Signal
E
Edit — Partial hallucination
The impact is directionally correct but contains inaccurate content, wrong stakeholder mapping, or a current/future state that collapses.
D
Delete — Primary signal
The impact should not exist. The AI generated content with no grounding in the source documents.
K
Keep — Quality baseline
The impact is accurate, complete, and trusted as-is. The baseline for output quality.

What the data will tell us — and what comes next

If practitioners are editing or deleting a significant portion of impacts, two failure modes need to be distinguished:

Low accuracy
The agent is extracting real impacts but getting the content wrong — wrong stakeholder mapping, collapsed current/future state, inaccurate field content. This is a prompt engineering or output format problem.
Hallucinations or assumptions
The agent is generating impacts that have no grounding in the source documents — fabrications or gap-filling. This is a model behavior problem that requires a different intervention.

If the data points to either failure mode, the next phase is a controlled experiment — research with the engineering team on the underlying technology, followed by targeted interventions to increase accuracy and reduce hallucination rates. The quality test is designed to tell us not just whether the output is good, but precisely where it breaks and why.

"Would you commit this output to a client deliverable as-is, with edits, or not yet?"

UXR renders the prototype in Claude before sessions begin. Raw session data goes through DMP — atomic observations, inductive coding, synthesis. Findings are integrated back into Claude, which renders the final iterated prototype. That artifact is what the 90-min workshop runs on.
01
No more decks. No more Figma comment threads.
PMs were in the US. Design was in India. 50+ Figma comments were piling up with no resolution path — and a time zone gap that made async back-and-forth unsustainable. Research findings were ingested back into a rendered Claude prototype — not a PowerPoint. One 90-min session replaced the entire comment thread. PMs, design, and engineering in one room, one shared artifact, one moment of decision.
02
The 90 minutes focused on one question: is this feasible?
With a 6-day sprint and a US/India time zone gap, there was no room for async back-and-forth. Users ask for things that aren't always possible — infrastructure, technology, capacity are real constraints. The workshop collapsed what would have been weeks of Figma comments and delayed decisions into one session: what can we actually build, and what do we do about the gap. PM, design, and engineering left with answers, not open threads.
03
DMP extracted the findings. Claude rendered the prototype. The workshop made the decision.
Raw session data went through DMP — atomic observations, inductive coding, synthesis. Those findings were ingested back into Claude, which rendered the next iteration of the prototype with user feedback built in. That Claude-rendered prototype was the artifact for the 90-min workshop. Not a deck. Not a Figma file with comments. A live, iterated prototype showing exactly what users said and what changed because of it. PM, design, and engineering used that session to decide what was feasible and what wasn't.
"PMs in the US. Design in India. 50+ Figma comments, a time zone gap, and a 6-day sprint that wasn't planned to include UXR. I cut the async loop entirely — one 90-min session with a Claude-rendered prototype and real user feedback. That was the handoff."
A similar organizational redesign — moving design from a reactive service to a strategic capability — is documented in CS03 · Design Ops Transformation. Two different engagements. The same underlying move.
The Larger Move

From UX researcher to AI Experience Designer with product influence.

Phase 01
Established domain authority. The feature prioritization evidence base the entire engineering roadmap was built on. The finding that mandatory features needed defending changed how the product team treated the feature set.
Phase 02
Expanded scope to end-to-end product definition. Moved the conversation from "what features do we build" to "what navigation decisions must be resolved before a single line of code is written."
Phase 03
AI leadership positioning made explicit. The hallucination detection framework built into the methodology is not a standard UX research construct. It is a behavioral proxy for AI output trustworthiness.
